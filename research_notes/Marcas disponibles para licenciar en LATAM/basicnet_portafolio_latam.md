# BasicNet S.p.A. brand portfolio: licensing and distribution status in Latin America and the Caribbean (as of November 2026)

Research date: 26 November 2026. Primary sources: BasicNet's 2024 and 2025 half-year reports, the 2025 Annual Accounts (published 26 March 2026), the conference-call decks of 12 February 2026 and 30 April 2026, and Investing.com transcripts of the H1 2026 (31 July 2026) and Q3/9M 2026 (21 October 2026) calls. Country-level licensee names come mostly from brand websites and trade or local press. Many of those sources date from 2013 to 2022 and are flagged as possibly outdated.

Brand portfolio at 31 December 2025, per the 2025 Annual Accounts: Kappa, Robe di Kappa, K-Way, Superga, Briko, Jesus Jeans, Sabelt, Sebago, Woolrich (from 1 December 2025, rights for Europe only) and Sundek (acquired 16 December 2025) — [BasicNet 2025 Annual Accounts & Sustainability Statement, pub. 26/03/2026](https://www.basicnet.com/contenuti/reports/bilanci/BasicNet_2025_Annual_Accounts_Sustainability_Statement.pdf)

## 1. Who are the current Latin American licensees for Kappa, K-Way, Superga, Sebago, Briko and Jesus Jeans, by country?

### Takeaway
Kappa is the only BasicNet brand with a documented licensee network in Latin America. Confirmed or likely current Kappa licensees: SPR Indústria de Confecção (Brazil, apparel), JRT Marcas Brasil (Brazil, footwear), Distrinando S.A. (Argentina), and Grupo Pernine (Colombia and the Andean/Caribbean region, last confirmed in 2022). New Kappa agreements were signed in 2025 for Mexico, the Caribbean and Uruguay, but the counterparties are not public. Superga had LATAM partners, last confirmed 2013–2018 (Distrinando in Argentina, HeroBrands in Peru, Pernine or HeroBrands in Colombia, Brandshouse/Idistribution in Brazil), and in 2026 BasicNet said Superga is moving to direct distribution worldwide. I found no current LATAM licensee for K-Way, Sebago, Briko, Jesus Jeans, Robe di Kappa (beyond the Kappa licences), Sundek or Woolrich.

### Inferences
- The 2025 "new agreements" for Kappa in Mexico, the Caribbean and Uruguay suggest the old regional set-up was split up. Uruguay was once bundled with Distrinando's Argentina licence, and the Caribbean was once inside Pernine's Andean/Caribbean scope. Who now holds these territories is not public.
- Brazil (SPR and JRT, with a club deal to 2030) and Argentina (Distrinando, still self-describing as exclusive licensee in 2026) look like the most clearly occupied Kappa territories in LATAM.
- The Pernine Andean licence is unverified after 2022. It is the main uncertainty for Colombia, Ecuador and Venezuela.
- The password-locked kappa.pe store and the absence of a Chilean licensee suggest Peru and Chile may be weakly covered or uncovered for Kappa. This is an inference, not confirmed by BasicNet.
- Because Basic Properties America is being liquidated, every Americas licence for Kappa, Superga and Sebago will be reassigned to a new contracting entity during 2026. That is a natural point for renegotiating or not renewing contracts.

### Gaps
- No public source names the 2025 Kappa licensees for Mexico, the Caribbean or Uruguay, or the North American "new trading partner".
- Contract start and expiry dates are not public for any current LATAM licence. The only known dates are the 2013 Distrinando six-year deal, the 2013–2021 SPR term (since renewed), and Pernine from 2018.
- I could not verify whether Pernine still holds Kappa (Andean region) or Superga (Colombia) in 2026.
- No current data found on Superga operators in Brazil, Chile, Peru or Colombia after 2019, or on which "couple of licenses" Superga kept.
- No evidence found for any brand in Bolivia, Paraguay, or individual Central American countries.
- No official BasicNet licensee directory exists on basicnet.com. The site has no public licensee list, and probes for /licensees and /network returned 404.

## 2. Which brand/territory combinations have no licensee, were recently terminated, or are being offered?

### Takeaway
BasicNet says publicly that it terminated "the previous agreement we had for Kappa". On the Q3 2026 call this is discussed alongside the Americas, the "market with the most negative sign". Management also said it chose "not to rush and pick some other licensee just for the sake of having one" and is "working to find the right partner". The exact territory is not named. BasicNet's stated strategy is to extend territorial coverage "through finding new qualified licensees – particularly for more recently acquired brands". Apart from Kappa, I found no active licensee in most LATAM countries for K-Way, Sebago, Briko, Jesus Jeans or Sundek. Superga is being pulled back to direct, selective distribution, and Woolrich is not available because BasicNet owns only the European rights.

## 4. How does BasicNet's licensing model work, and what restrictions would a Kappa licensee face in taking another BasicNet brand or a competing brand?

### Takeaway
BasicNet runs a "network" Business System with two kinds of partner. Commercial licensees are independent business owners with territorial distribution licences who pay royalties, with guaranteed minimums paid in advance and backed by guarantee deposits. Productive licensees (Sourcing Centers) manufacture for those licensees and pay BasicNet sourcing commissions. Each brand is licensed by its own brand-owning company. A Kappa licence therefore does not carry rights to other brands; each needs a separate contract with a different counterparty (Superga S.r.l., Sebago S.r.l., K-Way S.p.A., now 40% Permira). Licensees must follow group marketing guidelines and buy through the Sourcing Center platform. I found no public text of non-compete clauses. In practice, LATAM licensees have held several BasicNet brands (Distrinando: Kappa and Superga; Pernine: Kappa and reportedly Superga) alongside competing sportswear brands (Distrinando also holds Reebok and 361; Pernine "distribuye otras marcas").

### Cited Findings

**Business System**
- BasicNet developed "a 'network' business model, targeting licensees as the ideal partner for the development, distribution and sourcing of its products globally". BasicNet S.p.A. provides "Powered by" services (IT, coordination of production and commercial information flows on the licensee network, strategic finance). The brand-owning companies handle R&D and global marketing. "The brand-owning companies in the directly managed territories and the licensees, according to territory or goods category, distribute products to retailers, carry out local marketing, regional logistics and working capital funding." — [2025 Annual Accounts](https://www.basicnet.com/contenuti/reports/bilanci/BasicNet_2025_Annual_Accounts_Sustainability_Statement.pdf)
- "The production flows of the Group brand finished products which are distributed by the commercial licensee businesses in their territories are awarded to the licensee businesses (the Sourcing Centers), managed through the Business System platform" — [2025 Annual Accounts](https://www.basicnet.com/contenuti/reports/bilanci/BasicNet_2025_Annual_Accounts_Sustainability_Statement.pdf)
- Sourcing Centers' sales "are only made to commercial licensees or entities within the 'Powered by BasicNet' scope. The production licenses issued to the Sourcing Centers, differing from those issued to the commercial licensees, do not have regional limitations, but are rather based on technical production and business competences." Sales by Sourcing Centers to commercial licensees are made before the licensees sell to end customers — [2025 Annual Accounts](https://www.basicnet.com/contenuti/reports/bilanci/BasicNet_2025_Annual_Accounts_Sustainability_Statement.pdf)

**Glossary (investor decks)**
- Commercial licensees are "independent business owners, granted licenses to distribute Group brand products in their respective regions" and generate royalties. Productive licensees (sourcing centers) are "third-party entities whose function is to manufacture goods branded with the Group brands for the exclusive sale to Commercial licensees or to Group companies" and generate sourcing commissions — [Conference call 12/02/2026](https://www.basicnet.com/contenuti/reports/DocInfoAnnuali/Conference_Call_12.02.26.pdf); [Conference call 30/04/2026](https://www.basicnet.com/contenuti/reports/DocInfoAnnuali/Conference_Call_30.04.2026.pdf)

**Minimum guarantees and deposits**
- "the commercial license contracts usually establish the advance payment of guaranteed minimum royalties"; economic conditions can hit licensees' financial capacity, "particularly where such licensees had previously exceeded the guaranteed minimums" — [2025 Annual Accounts, risk section](https://www.basicnet.com/contenuti/reports/bilanci/BasicNet_2025_Annual_Accounts_Sustainability_Statement.pdf); [H1 2024 report](https://www.basicnet.com/contenuti/reports/relazionisemestrali/BasicNet_Group_2024_Half_Year_Report.pdf)
- "The 'guarantee deposits' include the guarantees received from licensees, to cover the minimum royalties guaranteed contractually." — [H1 2024 report, notes](https://www.basicnet.com/contenuti/reports/relazionisemestrali/BasicNet_Group_2024_Half_Year_Report.pdf)
- Historical LATAM example (2013): the six-year Distrinando licence for Argentina and Uruguay reportedly set minimum royalties on a minimum turnover of about US$57m, with a sales target of about US$73.4m, and included local manufacturing. These figures were seen only in a search summary of 2013 coverage and should be verified — [El Cronista (2013)](https://www.cronista.com/negocios/la-italiana-kappa-comienza-a-fabricar-en-la-argentina/); [SGI Europe (2013)](https://www.sgieurope.com/basicnet-has-entered-into-a-six-year-partnership-with-an-argentinian-/40529.article)

**Royalties versus direct sales**
- Royalties and sourcing commissions are reported together: €66.4m in FY2025, about €27m in H1 2026 — [2025 Annual Accounts](https://www.basicnet.com/contenuti/reports/bilanci/BasicNet_2025_Annual_Accounts_Sustainability_Statement.pdf); [H1 2026 transcript](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-basicnet-h1-2026-sales-rise-as-profit-is-hit-by-costs-93CH-4829075)

### Inferences
- A Kappa licensee wanting another BasicNet brand would negotiate a separate licence with that brand's owning company, each with its own guaranteed minimum royalty. LATAM precedent (Distrinando, Pernine) shows BasicNet does award several brands to the same partner in a territory.
- For K-Way, Permira's 40% stake and the new K-Way management (CEO since September 2026) probably add a separate approval layer and a Europe-first strategy. That likely makes new LATAM K-Way licences harder to obtain in the short term.
- LATAM licensees holding Reebok, 361 and Crocs next to Kappa suggests BasicNet has not imposed blanket bans on competing brands in these contracts, though specific category exclusivities may exist.
- Because products must be bought from approved Sourcing Centers through the BasicNet platform (or made locally under an explicit manufacturing right, as in Argentina and Colombia), a licensee's margin structure includes the sourcing commission built into the Sourcing Center's price, on top of the royalty.

### Gaps
- Royalty rates, sourcing-commission percentages, typical licence term, and minimum-guarantee levels for current contracts are not disclosed.
- No public contract text was found on non-compete or competing-brand clauses, exclusivity, change-of-control, or rights of first refusal for additional BasicNet brands.
- Whether Kappa licensees must use BasicNet Sourcing Centers exclusively, or may manufacture locally by default, varies by contract. Local manufacturing rights are documented only for Argentina (2013) and Colombia (c. 2022).
